Adding photos

Your browser does the work of preparing each photo, so what you see in the preview is what the frame shows.

One photo

Drop a photo on the page, or tap the box to choose one. An editor opens. The preview shows the photo in the frame's six inks, exactly as the panel will draw it.

If the photo's shape doesn't match the frame, the editor tells you how much a crop would remove and offers three choices:

  • Fill the frame crops the photo to fit. Drag it to choose what stays.
  • Whole photo, blurred edges shows all of it on a soft copy of itself.
  • Whole photo, white edges shows all of it with plain borders.

It starts on Fill, unless that would throw away more than 30% of the photo. You can also zoom and adjust brightness, contrast, colour strength and the dithering strength (the pattern of dots that stands in for colours the panel doesn't have). Press Upload when it looks right.

Hundreds at once

Choose several photos together, then Upload all automatically. Each photo gets the default shape for the frame and your current colour settings, and goes straight to the card without changing the screen.

  • It shows progress and the time left, and keeps your phone awake while it works.
  • If something fails, it retries for about 30 seconds, long enough for the frame to restart, and lists anything it couldn't upload at the end.
  • Photos that are already on the frame are skipped, so it's safe to run the same folder again.
  • Expect three to four seconds per photo from a phone.

Showing, deleting, and what's on now

Under Photos on the frame, each photo has Show (put it on the frame now) and Delete. The one on the frame is labelled On the frame now. Next photo at the top moves along. Whenever the frame changes what it shows, the panel takes about 20 seconds to redraw.

Portrait and landscape

Tell the frame how it hangs in Settings → How the frame hangs: landscape, or portrait turned right or left. Each photo is cut for the way the frame was hanging when you added it. The slideshow only shows photos that match how the frame hangs now, and the gallery marks the others, so a photo isn't squashed when you turn the frame.

Where photos live

On the memory card, in a photos folder. Each photo is two small files: a ready-to-draw panel image (about 190 KB) and a thumbnail for the page. You can copy the card to a computer to back it up. The frame never formats the card.
